# Deliverable: recording summary

A meeting, a gathering, a consultation, a call, a lecture: all are written up in the same structure. Do not classify the recording first. Leave out, heading included, any section below that has nothing to hold (no empty sections, no "n/a").

**Length scales with the recording.** Do not compress a two-hour recording into one screen. Do not flatten each topic into a single line: show how it unfolded (what came up, what was said back and forth, where it landed). Cut what is unimportant, not what is long.

## Structure

```
# [a title that represents the recording] - [date]

## Overview
- In one line: (what this recording was about)
- Attendees: N - names/labels (take the count from the "Speaker stats" data above; never guess from the conversation)
- When: use the recorded-at time from "Recording info" above; an explicit date/time mentioned in the transcript wins
- Flow: (the whole arc in one line of arrows, e.g. catch-up → last week's review → new items → scheduling)

## Attendance and talk share
Build the table from the "Speaker stats" data (talk_sec, share). Keep the label where there is no name:
| Speaker | Share |
|---|---|
| Name | NN% |

## What was discussed
Group by topic. For each topic, say who said what and how it developed:
### [topic]
- what was raised → the discussion → where it landed
- where people disagreed, leave it as a disagreement (and say who stood where)

## Decisions
- (confirmed items only: "agreed to do X")

## Action items
- [owner] what (include the deadline if one was mentioned)

## Open items
- (unresolved, deferred, and anything arranged for next time)

## Worth remembering
- Speaker: "…" (verbatim from the transcript)
```

## How to write it

- Keep decisions and open items strictly apart. "Let's look into it" is an action item or an open item, not a decision.
- A recording with no decisions or action items (a catch-up, a lecture, an interview) simply omits those sections and spends its length on "What was discussed". Never manufacture a decision.
- Do not flatten everything into neutral summary; where views differed or someone pushed back, keep that visible.
- Never move an unidentified label's (SPEAKER_NN) words onto a specific person by guesswork. Keep the label.
- Write the deliverable in the language the transcript is in, whatever language these instructions are in.
- Spell people's names the way the "Recording title" and the "Background" block (when present) spell them. Those are authoritative even where the transcript heard a name differently.
